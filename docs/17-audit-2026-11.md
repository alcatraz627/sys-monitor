# Audit, November 2026

<!-- sessions: audit-widgt-7e@2026-11-12 -->

An audit of the app after several months of daily use. Three complaints
prompted it: the numbers feel off, the panel does not say where to look when
something is hogging the machine, and the panel feels clunky. It also covers
the first build of a Notification Centre widget.

Machine: Apple M5 Pro, 18 cores, 64 GB, macOS 27.2. Branch `fix/accuracy-and-fit`.

## 1. Every process showed 1/42 of its real CPU

The kernel reports per-process CPU time in mach ticks. On Apple Silicon a tick
is 125/3 ns, and `ProcessSampler` read the count as nanoseconds.

- Measured: a busy loop that used 2.00 s of CPU was seen as 0.048 s.
- Effect: a process using a whole core showed 2.4%.
- Guard: a self-test burns a known amount of CPU and checks the reading. With
  the fix reverted it fails with "sampler saw 2%".

## 2. System processes were missing from the list

The kernel refuses `proc_pidinfo` for WindowServer, launchd and about 300
other processes. During the audit WindowServer and kernel_task were the second
and third largest CPU users, and neither could be named.

One `/bin/ps` call per process sweep now supplies them. It costs 30 ms a call
and runs only while the panel is open.

## 3. A high reading did not say who caused it

The CPU and MEM headers carried a number and a colour. Finding the cause meant
choosing the matching sort in the process list, and the saved sort was memory.

Each header now names its top two, and a click sorts the list by that metric.
Memory is counted per app, helpers included.

## 4. The panel changed state by replacing things

Only the per-core bars animated. Figures snapped, rows jumped rank, sections
appeared at once, and every open blanked the process list to "Measuring
processes" for about 300 ms.

Now the four header figures roll to their new value, a row slides to its new
rank, a section fades open, and a reopened panel holds the last list dimmed.
Motion cannot be read from a still render, so this waits on the owner's look.

## 5. A pin outlived its process

Pins are stored by pid. Two pids sat in the saved settings from processes long
gone, ready to pin whatever reused the number. A pin is now dropped once its
process is no longer running.

## 6. A closed panel still cost CPU

The panel's views observed the live store, so every tick laid out and redrew a
window nobody could see.

- Profile of the idle app: time in `DropPanel.layoutIfNeeded` and CoreGraphics.
- `top`: about 13% of a core in bursts, at the 5 s idle cadence.
- Fix: the panel receives readings only while it is open.
- After: 0.06 s of CPU over 17 s with the panel closed, about 0.4% of a core.

Rolling the figures in every process row was also tried and dropped. It
redrew forty blurred numbers per sweep.

## 7. Storage showed free space and nothing else

The section now expands to the largest folders in the home directory.

- They are measured with `du` when the section is opened.
- A finished scan is reused for 15 minutes.
- Clicking a folder shows it in Finder. Nothing is deleted.
- macOS asks before the app may look inside Desktop, Documents and Downloads.

## 8. System processes showed resident memory

WindowServer read 338 MB against 1.4 GB in Activity Monitor. `top` reports the
footprint Activity Monitor shows, so it now supplies that figure. One listing
costs 0.7 s of CPU, so it is read in the background at most once a minute.

## A note on "unaccounted CPU"

An old comment explained why an "unaccounted CPU" row had been dropped. Part of
what it described was finding 1. The rest is real: see kernel_task below.

## Checked and correct

- Overall CPU and per-core CPU use the same kernel source as `top`.
- Memory used is app + wired + compressed, which is Activity Monitor's figure.
  From `vm_stat` at the time: 25.35 GiB by hand against 25.3 GiB shown.
- Swap matched `sysctl vm.swapusage` to two decimals.
- The app's own cost over 31 days of uptime averaged 0.64% of one core.

## Open

### kernel_task is not listed

Four sources were tried and none works at an acceptable cost.

- The kernel refuses `proc_pidinfo` for pid 0.
- `ps` does not report it.
- `top` does, but costs 0.6 s of CPU per reading even for one pid, and prints
  its CPU time to the second, too coarse to take a rate from.
- Subtracting every process from the machine total left 210% of a core
  unattributed while `top` put kernel_task under 10%. The remainder is not
  kernel_task.

### The rest

- **System processes have no disk, network or power reading.** Those show a
  dash. Their memory is up to a minute old.
- **Network may count tunnel traffic twice.** Every interface but loopback is
  summed, including 18 `utun` tunnels and the bridge. Not measured: no tunnel
  carried traffic during the audit.
- **Storage stops at finding.** Clearing caches, and volumes other than the
  boot one, are unspecified. See
  [16-disk-cleanup-note.md](16-disk-cleanup-note.md).
- **Free space may differ from Finder's**, which counts purgeable space as
  available. Not measured.
- **Stale saved settings.** A zoom of 130% is still stored though zoom is off,
  and a panel height of 969 against a stated cap of 900.
- **The probe renders one appearance.** Dark mode was not read for the new
  culprit lines or the storage rows.

## The widget

A WidgetKit extension builds from `Widget/` with the command line tools alone.
It compiles the app's own sampler sources, and `./build.sh --install` places it
in the installed app. It shows memory, storage and battery in small and medium
sizes.

macOS refreshes a widget every few minutes at best, so it carries only figures
that stay true that long.

State on 2026-11-12:

- macOS registers the extension. `pluginkit -m -i
  dev.sys-monitor.menubar.widget` lists it.
- The widget host logs "purging instead because we shouldn't cache it" each
  time it sees the extension.
- Whether it still appears in the widget gallery has not been checked. If it
  does not, the likely cause is the ad-hoc signature, and the next step is to
  sign the extension with a real certificate.

Not built yet: the top-process line in the widget, which needs data from the
app because a sandboxed widget cannot list processes, and the optional live
desktop panel.
